# Flood-response DT SoS simulator requirements

## 1. Purpose

The simulator shall support controlled experiments that compare how digital-twin (DT) resilience profiles affect mission success, constellation membership, rescue capacity, and resource use during a flood-response mission.

The resilience profiles, ordered from low to high resilience, are:

| Profile | Resilience |
|---|---|
| Reactive | Low |
| Proactive | Medium-low |
| Controlled | Medium-high |
| Adaptive | High |

## 2. Generated state-machine constraint

**This is a mandatory implementation constraint.** Files in `generated/` are autogenerated by itemis CREATE and shall never be edited manually or regenerated as part of ordinary simulator changes. They are authoritative for profile-specific state-transition behavior.

The simulator, coordinator, and digital-twin adapter shall use the generated state machines to trigger and observe transitions in both regions:

- Health-region events and states shall be driven and observed through the generated machine's `hm` interface.
- Belonging-region events and states shall be driven and observed through the generated machine's `bm` interface.
- Profile-specific effects of health on belonging shall be encoded in the corresponding generated state machine. External orchestration may raise events and observe states, but shall not duplicate or override those transition rules.
- When changing profile machines, the simulator shall preserve or replay the necessary state through generated events, without editing generated files.

## 3. Policy strategies and experimental configurations

The paper-facing strategy terminology is **Static permissive**, **Static restrictive**, and **Dynamic**. The implementation retains the stable identifiers `low_only`, `high_only`, and `mixed` for configuration and output compatibility; these identifiers map to the three paper-facing names in that order. “Profile” refers to one resilience level (Reactive, Proactive, Controlled, or Adaptive), while “policy strategy” describes how profiles are assigned over a run.

**REQ-1 — Configurable profiles.** The simulator shall allow researchers to define and select multiple configurations for profile behavior. Each configuration shall have a stable identifier and its parameter values shall be recorded for reproducibility.

**REQ-2 — Assignment strategies.** The simulator shall support these strategies:

1. **Static permissive** (`low_only`): all DTs use Reactive.
2. **Static restrictive** (`high_only`): all DTs use Adaptive.
3. **Dynamic** (`mixed`): DT profiles change by role as configured water-level thresholds are crossed.

All three strategies shall exercise both physical-health events through `hm` and independent Poisson-driven SoS membership requests through `bm`. Strategies differ in profile assignment, not in whether either transition cause is active. The causes and their outcomes shall be recorded separately.

**REQ-3 — Dynamic profile progression.** The Dynamic strategy shall support the following progression:

1. Operational DTs begin in Reactive. Boats held in reserve shall use the reserve profile specified for the strategy; under Dynamic, reserve boats remain Reactive while waiting to join the mission.
2. At the first threshold, the operational drone becomes Proactive and remains Proactive at later thresholds. Operational boats follow the configured boat progression.
3. At the second threshold, operational boats become Controlled.
4. At the final threshold, operational rescue boats become Adaptive. Monitoring boats do not become Adaptive unless assigned a rescue role.
5. The pumping station progresses from Reactive to Proactive at the first threshold and to Controlled at the second threshold. It does not become Adaptive in the Dynamic strategy.
6. When a reserve boat is activated, it shall leave the reserve assignment and adopt the profile specified for its assigned operational role at the current water-level threshold. Its profile, role, activation time, and resulting membership transitions shall be recorded.

Thresholds and role-to-profile mappings shall be configurable and recorded for each run. The current synthetic baseline uses Dynamic thresholds of 0.5 m (Proactive), 1.0 m (Controlled), and 1.8 m (Adaptive for rescue boats); these are scenario-tuning assumptions, not site-calibrated limits. A boat that returns to reserve status shall use the reserve profile for its strategy (Reactive under Dynamic). “Static restrictive” means all DTs, including the pumping station and reserve boats, use Adaptive throughout the mission. Reserve belonging and health behavior is specified in REQ-5A and REQ-15C.

## 4. DT roles and boat fleet

**REQ-4 — Separate roles from physical type.** The simulator shall distinguish logical boat roles (rescue and monitoring) from the physical boat type. Boats may share the same physical model while being assigned different roles and profiles.

**REQ-5 — Model active and reserve boats.** The simulator shall support multiple equivalent boats and configurable active and reserve status. The current baseline fleet shall include exactly six reserve boats in every strategy and profile configuration; all six shall be present in Static permissive, Static restrictive, and Dynamic runs. All strategies shall use the same demand-based reserve activation rule. Under Static restrictive and Dynamic, reserve boats may additionally replace a DT that leaves Active membership due to an announced generated health response. Reactive health changes shall not trigger a repair or health-based replacement, and a Reactive DT that remains Active shall continue occupying its role despite task failures. Under Dynamic, reserve boats shall use Reactive while waiting and adopt the current role-specific profile for the Dynamic assignment when activated, then return to Reactive when placed back in reserve. The reserve-pool size and unit configuration shall be recorded with experiment outputs.

**REQ-5A — Initialize and maintain unused backup boats in Disengaged and Ideal.** Every designated backup boat shall start in the generated belonging state `Disengaged` and generated health state `Ideal`. While it remains unused in reserve, it shall remain Disengaged and Ideal, shall not accumulate flood/rain fault exposure, and shall not perform operational mission tasks. When activated, the coordinator shall request the generated belonging transitions through `bm`; the boat shall leave Disengaged and enter `Prepared` before proceeding toward mission membership. Health exposure shall begin only after it leaves backup status and is activated for mission service. The simulator shall not directly assign generated states. Backup availability, profile, activation request, and any rejected or delayed state request shall be recorded.

**REQ-6 — Record boat assignments.** The simulator shall record each boat's role, availability, profile, departure or replacement time, and rescue or monitoring activity.

## 5. Autonomous SoS requests and minimum rescue capacity

**REQ-7 — Poisson-driven SoS requests.** In every strategy, each eligible DT shall be able to make an autonomous SoS-related request to change constellation membership, independently of health. Request rates shall be configurable by profile and/or DT role, expressed per simulated minute in the baseline configuration, and recorded with their units. A request is a membership decision stimulus, not an unconditional command to leave.

**REQ-8 — Poisson-process timing and virtual simulation clock.** The simulation clock shall use seconds as its base unit, while the default mission step shall be 60 simulated seconds (one minute); a finer step may be selected when required by task, deadline, or state-machine timing. The baseline mission horizon shall be 60 simulated minutes, configurable up to at least 120 simulated minutes. Mission duration, step, communication latency, fault exposure, task durations, repair/retry delays, victim survival times, and event rates shall use consistent, explicitly recorded units. Generated state-machine timers expressed in milliseconds shall be converted to seconds at the timer-service boundary. Each Poisson request configuration shall specify its rate `lambda` and time unit. Event counts over interval `delta_t` shall follow `Poisson(lambda * delta_t)`, or an equivalent exponential inter-arrival implementation. For a discrete step, the probability of one or more requests may be calculated as `1 - exp(-lambda * delta_t)`. The simulator shall not treat a Poisson-distributed count as a direct time-to-departure sample. Simulated time is virtual: advancing one simulated minute shall not require one minute of wall-clock execution.

**REQ-9 — Profile-mediated membership response and announcement.** A Poisson request shall not directly mutate belonging state. The assigned profile's membership policy shall determine whether a join/leave request is submitted, accepted, denied, or delayed; any resulting state transition shall be triggered and observed through the generated `bm` API. Higher-resilience profiles shall be configured to handle SoS requests more conservatively, including the ability to deny or delay some voluntary leave requests and to control admission. Health events go through `hm`; only the generated profile machine may translate those events into belonging transitions. In particular, Reactive is announcement-only: health changes do not change belonging under Reactive. The SoS shall be notified of a DT joining, leaving, or otherwise changing belonging only when the generated machine invokes its `announce` operation. Ordinary telemetry may report health and physical measurements but shall not expose belonging as an alternate membership-notification channel. State observations and logs may record unannounced health changes for measurement, but shall not treat them as SoS membership notifications. The coordinator shall not infer or queue a health departure from a health event that left belonging unchanged.

**REQ-10 — Protect mission capacity for voluntary departures.** Before submitting an accepted voluntary leave request, the simulator shall check whether the remaining available fleet can meet rescue demand before the victim survival deadline. Effective boat capacity shall account for the task success probability associated with each boat's current health state; a degraded boat shall contribute less expected capacity than an Ideal boat. Capacity-based deferral/denial shall be recorded separately from profile-based denial and generated-machine rejection. A health-driven withdrawal shall not be blocked by this voluntary-departure capacity check.

**REQ-11 — Calculate the minimum required boats.** Minimum rescue demand shall depend on remaining victims, time until the survival deadline, boat rescue throughput (victims per simulated minute), and flooded area. Capacity calculations shall scale consistently with the configured step duration; a finer step shall not change the represented per-minute boat throughput. The demand-based activation rule shall be identical in Static permissive, Static restrictive, and Dynamic: if the number of occupied rescue roles is below the required number and additional eligible monitoring or reserve boats are available, the coordinator shall request them through the generated belonging interface. A DT that remains Active occupies its role even when its health reduces task success; health-weighted task outcomes shall be measured separately and shall not, by themselves, create a demand-based reserve request. Static restrictive and Dynamic may additionally request health replacements following REQ-16D. Minimum effective capacity and mission outcomes shall still account for the health-dependent task success probability. The calculation and assumptions shall be documented. A starting form is:

`minimum_boats(t) = ceil(remaining_victims(t) / expected_rescues_per_boat(flooded_area(t), time_until_deadline))`

where `expected_rescues_per_boat` is the expected number of victims one available boat can rescue before the deadline, given flooded area and remaining time. The victims-per-area ratio may be reported as a demand or difficulty indicator, but alone does not determine the number of boats required.

## 6. Physical health, generated transitions, and repair

**REQ-12 — Profile-dependent health response.** Flood/rain exposure shall affect the physical twin and produce health events through `hm`. The assigned profile's generated machine alone shall determine whether, when, and how those health events change belonging through `bm`. Reactive health changes do not affect belonging: a Reactive DT, including one in Failed health, remains in its current belonging state and continues its mission attempts, which fail according to its health-dependent task probability. Reactive health changes do not initiate repair or health-based replacement; however, Static permissive remains eligible for the same demand-based reserve activation rule as other strategies. Under Proactive, Controlled, and Adaptive, when the generated machine responds to deteriorating health by announcing a departure from Active membership, that DT becomes unavailable for its prior role, is repaired while out of Active service, and a distinct reserve may take over the saved role. For Adaptive, when the generated machine reports `RestrictedRole` while the DT is Active, the adapter shall raise the generated `bm.uncertainty_exceeded` event. The generated machine shall decide whether this starts a deferred exit; the simulator shall not assign belonging directly. A DT in an announced health-triggered deferred exit shall not receive new mission tasks while that exit is pending. Health shall not otherwise be converted directly into a membership command by orchestration code. Every profile strategy shall experience the same configured physical stress model under matched seeds and conditions, so profile differences arise from profile behavior rather than different fault injection.

**REQ-13 — Distinguish transition causes and SoS notifications.** Logs and reports shall identify (a) health events and any resulting health-driven belonging changes, (b) Poisson SoS requests and their responses, and (c) capacity-based deferrals. Health-related belonging transitions shall never be sampled from the Poisson process. The SoS shall process a membership notification only when it corresponds to a generated `announce` callback and an actual belonging-state change. Health-only changes, including Reactive health transitions, shall not create a pending health departure or later be reclassified as a health-caused leave. If a generated profile machine explicitly enters an announced, health-triggered deferred-exit state while belonging remains Active (for example, Adaptive `PendingExit`), the adapter shall preserve that cause as pending metadata. It shall trigger health-departure bookkeeping only if the generated machine later announces an actual departure from Active; it shall clear the metadata if the generated machine cancels the deferred exit. The adapter shall not change the generated state or force the departure.

**REQ-14 — Permit health-driven operational unavailability.** A physical twin that becomes unsafe or inoperable may become unavailable even if remaining capacity falls below the mission minimum. The simulator shall record the capacity shortfall and its effect on mission outcome. Any belonging transition shall still be determined by its generated machine.

**REQ-15 — Inject progressive environmental faults.** The simulator shall accumulate flood-water exposure for each operational physical twin using a shared, configurable water-level threshold (metres in the baseline synthetic scenario), an exposure rate per simulated minute, and an exposure interval per health transition. The current synthetic baseline uses a 1.5 m threshold, an exposure rate of 1.2 units per simulated minute, and 10 accumulated exposure units per health transition. These values are tuning assumptions, not calibrated asset failure limits. Exposure shall be integrated over elapsed simulated time, so changing the simulation step does not change the physical exposure represented by the same incident duration. When accumulated exposure reaches the configured interval, it shall raise the corresponding next-stage event through the generated `hm` interface; it shall not directly assign a generated health state. Unused reserve units shall not accumulate exposure. These environmental fault parameters shall be identical across resilience profiles in matched experiments unless a separately stated experiment explicitly varies physical protection.

**REQ-15A — Health-dependent task outcomes.** Health shall affect mission-task execution independently of generated belonging state. The initial per-attempt success probabilities shall decrease linearly from 100% at `Ideal` to 0% at `Failed`, across the ordered health states:

| Health state | Task success probability |
|---|---:|
| Ideal | 100% |
| Defective | 83.3% (5/6) |
| Faulty | 66.7% (4/6) |
| Erroneous | 50.0% (3/6) |
| Malfunctioning | 33.3% (2/6) |
| Degraded | 16.7% (1/6) |
| Failed | 0% |

For states between the endpoints, each task attempt shall be sampled independently using a reproducible DT-specific random stream. These interpolated values are an explicit baseline assumption, not empirical reliability measurements, and shall be configurable and included in experiment output. An independently inoperable physical twin shall fail its task regardless of the health-state probability. Each task result shall identify the DT, task, health state, configured success probability, outcome, and failure reason. Every attempt shall be counted locally so message loss cannot omit it from run-level outcomes. Generated state machines remain authoritative for belonging; this requirement governs task execution, not membership transitions.

**REQ-15B — Health-related communication availability.** The pumping station's health telemetry and task-result reporting channel shall become unavailable at `Degraded` or `Failed` and return after successful physical repair and generated health recovery. This reporting outage shall not override the task-success probability: the simulator shall still execute and record a task attempt locally, and the task may succeed according to REQ-15A. The SoS may therefore hold an Active membership view and lack current pump telemetry/results while the pump continues to have a nonzero probability of completing a task at `Degraded`. Health-related reporting outages shall be measured separately from task outcomes and simulated network outages. Communication loss shall not directly mutate belonging or health states.

**REQ-15C — Protect backup health until activation.** A designated unused backup DT shall remain in generated health state `Ideal` while it is Disengaged in reserve and shall receive no mission-related flood/rain fault exposure. When activated, it shall transition from Disengaged to Prepared through generated `bm` events; exposure shall begin only after it leaves backup status to enter mission service. A repaired DT returned to the backup pool shall not be considered available until its generated health state is `Ideal` and its generated belonging state is `Disengaged`. The simulator shall record the time and events for backup activation, exposure start, repair completion, and return to backup status. This requirement does not permit direct assignment of generated health or belonging states.

**REQ-16 — Repair after profile-authorized departure.** A health-impaired Proactive, Controlled, or Adaptive DT that leaves Active membership through an announced generated health response shall request repair while out of Active service. Reactive health changes shall not initiate repair; a Reactive DT remaining Active, even in Failed health, remains in the constellation and continues to fail tasks. Repair shall restore the physical twin and raise the generated `hm.recovery` event. Any resulting health or belonging transitions shall be made by the generated machine; repair shall not directly force belonging. Repair requests, repair time, and completed repairs shall be recorded. Repair effort shall increase the estimated mission cost according to the maintenance cost driver.

**REQ-16A — Return repaired DTs to Disengaged backup status, not directly to Active.** Health degradation shall trigger the belonging transitions defined by the generated profile machine. A DT that reaches Failed health shall progress through any generated Passive or Prepared states and return to `Disengaged` as determined by that machine; the simulator shall not skip or force intermediate transitions. After repair, the coordinator shall raise the generated recovery event through `hm`. The repaired DT shall remain out of Active service and be returned to Disengaged backup status through generated `bm` events; it shall not automatically rejoin Active membership or displace the replacement. It shall be eligible for future activation only after the generated health machine reports `Ideal` and the belonging machine reports `Disengaged`. A boat returned to backup status shall use the strategy's reserve profile (Reactive under Dynamic). These rules apply in all three strategies. If the generated machine rejects or delays health recovery or return to Disengaged, the simulator shall record the outcome and retry at a configurable interval while the mission remains active. A DT that left only for autonomous SoS reasons shall not be treated as a health-repair replacement under this requirement.

**REQ-16B — Repair the pumping station in Prepared.** The pumping station shall begin its repair only when its generated belonging state is `Prepared`. If an eligible non-Reactive profile has left Active but remains Passive, the simulator may request the generated `bm.leave_so_s` event to reach Prepared; it shall start repair only if the generated machine accepts that transition. It shall not force a state change or edit generated code. On repair completion, the simulator shall raise `hm.recovery`; the repaired pump shall remain out of Active service until a later SoS request needs it, following REQ-16A. A Reactive pump that remains Active shall not be moved out of Active solely to enable repair and shall not be repaired as a health response. Its task outcomes shall follow REQ-15A, while health-related reporting outages shall follow REQ-15B.

**REQ-16C — Request and deploy backup boats.** In every strategy, when the number of occupied rescue roles is below the demand-based minimum and an eligible monitoring or reserve boat can fill the gap, the SoS shall request that boat through the generated `bm` interface. A health-impaired Reactive boat that remains Active shall continue occupying its role and shall not trigger reserve activation merely because its task success probability has decreased. In Static restrictive and Dynamic, an announced health-related departure from Active service shall additionally trigger the replacement request in REQ-16D, even if the demand-based minimum has not yet fallen below its threshold. An unused backup shall be Disengaged and Ideal before activation; the generated machine shall transition it through Prepared as it joins mission service. Backups shall be logged when requested and activated for rescue. Backup capacity shall be weighted by the boat's health-dependent task success probability after activation. Repairs and backup deployment shall improve available expected mission capacity, but shall affect reported success only through actual task outcomes; they shall not directly force success.

**REQ-16D — Replace a health-departed DT during repair.** In Static restrictive and Dynamic, whenever a DT leaves Active constellation membership because of an announced generated health-related transition, the coordinator shall create a replacement request recording the departed DT, its role at departure, and departure time. If the generated machine defers the departure through an explicitly announced health-triggered state such as Adaptive `PendingExit`, the coordinator shall retain the health cause and use it only when the generated machine announces the later Active departure. If the generated machine cancels that deferred exit, no health replacement shall be requested. The coordinator shall request a distinct eligible backup unit through the generated `bm` interface while the affected DT is being repaired. A candidate shall be a different DT from the one it replaces and shall be reserve, Disengaged, Ideal, operational, and connected. The replacement shall inherit the recorded role at departure, even if the original DT's role later changes while it is repaired. A rescue boat replacement shall take over rescue service. The replacement is successful only after the generated machine admits it to Active membership and it is able to operate in the recorded role. A repaired DT shall return to Disengaged standby and shall not fulfill its own pending replacement request. If no distinct backup is available, or the generated machine rejects/delays activation, the simulator shall record the capacity shortfall and unreplaced interval; it shall not force a state transition. In Static permissive, Reactive health changes do not generate health departure or replacement requests. Requests, generated-machine responses, repair intervals, replacement intervals, role handoffs, and any time without a replacement shall be recorded.

## 7. Mission outcome metrics

**REQ-17 — Victim survival deadline and matched arrival cohort.** Victims shall have a configured survival deadline after which they cannot count as rescued alive. If victims arrive at different times, each deadline shall be based on that victim's arrival time. The experiment shall use a fixed, profile-independent arrival schedule over the selected 60–120-minute incident horizon. The run shall continue through the latest deadline for the final scheduled cohort, or explicitly record any victims still unresolved at mission stop without counting them as rescued or silently treating them as expired. Victim count, arrival times, survival duration, and resulting deadlines shall be identical across profile and departure-rate conditions for matched runs. The mission shall stop dispatching tasks at its configured service horizon; endpoint events due at that time shall be processed before results are finalized.

**REQ-18 — Define mission success as an outcome.** Each run shall determine success only from observed mission outcomes: the number or fraction of victims rescued alive before their deadlines compared with a configured target. The success predicate shall not inspect the policy-strategy name, resilience profile, or profile strategy. All profiles shall be evaluated using the same target and matched mission conditions.

**REQ-19 — Incident duration, flood model, and success-rate hypotheses.** The baseline incident shall represent 60 simulated minutes, with experiments configurable to at least 120 simulated minutes. The default simulation step shall be one simulated minute, with finer steps allowed where event timing requires them; the SimPy virtual clock shall allow this horizon to execute without real-time waiting. The baseline synthetic incident shall begin at 0.2 m water depth and use a 50,000 m² base storage area that expands by 15,000 m² per metre of depth. External inflow shall be 3,500 m³/minute and a successful active pump shall remove up to 300 m³/minute. Storage shall be updated by inflow minus pumped outflow for each elapsed interval, and the storage-to-depth relation shall be consistent with the stated stage-area relation. The baseline environmental fault trigger shall be 2.0 m water depth, with exposure accumulating at one exposure unit per simulated minute and one health-state event per 10 accumulated exposure units. These values are explicit synthetic assumptions for a reproducible benchmark, not site-calibrated measurements. The common victim cohort shall contain 240 victims: 10 at time zero and five waves of 46 at 10-minute intervals from minute 10 through minute 50. Each victim shall have a 10-minute survival time, placing the final cohort deadline at the 60-minute endpoint. Flood forcing, initial conditions, victim demand, and physical asset assumptions shall be shared across matched strategy runs; profile behavior may affect pump availability and therefore modeled outflow. Flooded area shall be reported in hectares, water level in metres, and storage/pump volumes in cubic metres. For each experimental condition, success rate shall be successful runs divided by valid runs, with both counts reported. Mission load, fault progression, rescue capacity, and deadlines shall be calibrated to avoid all conditions trivially achieving 100% success; the success target shall be configured independently of profile and tested for sensitivity. Under matched conditions, the expected strategy hypothesis is that Static restrictive achieves the highest success rate and incurs the highest resilience-related cost; Static permissive has the lowest cost and lower success; Dynamic provides a favorable balance. This is a testable model hypothesis, not a profile-based success override. If repeated matched experiments do not show the expected pattern, mechanisms and assumptions shall be investigated and reported rather than forcing the result.

**REQ-19A — Separate boat and pump effects.** Boats shall be the direct mechanism that rescues victims. Pumping may affect flood storage/depth/area and thus boat operating conditions, but shall not directly rescue victims; its indirect effect on rescue throughput shall be independently configurable and calibrated so it does not overwhelm boat availability and capacity. Rescue capacity shall be specified as victims per boat per simulated minute and shall scale with step duration. The pump-to-rescue-area coefficient shall multiply flooded area in hectares. The benchmark sensitivity values shall be 0.04 and 0.12 per hectare for the area coefficient and 2.0 and 3.0 victims per boat-minute for rescue throughput; these remain illustrative parameters pending calibration. Each run shall record factor values, units, and factor-condition identifier; comparisons shall use matched seeds.

**REQ-20 — DT-count and participation metrics.** Each run shall record both:

- peak number of DTs attached to the SoS; and
- total DT participation time, summed across DTs.

The simulator shall also record Active membership time by DT and asset type, separately from attached time. It shall record pumping-station Active time and delivered pumping output so that a healthy but Passive pump is not counted as providing service.

If one primary DT-count metric is required, peak attached DT count shall be used; participation time shall be reported separately.

## 8. Cost and resource-use measure

**REQ-21 — Estimate cost with activity-based costing.** The simulator shall use an activity-based costing (ABC) structure adapted from Su et al. (2025), rather than treating DT-time as the cost itself. The cost object and estimation scope shall be stated (for example, DT service cost per mission, with shared development/infrastructure cost separately amortized across a declared number of missions). The estimate shall map resource consumption to DT data activities and allocate activity costs to the DTs/scenarios that consume them.

Activity cost shall be estimated from consumed driver quantity multiplied by an applicable resource rate, with the allocation and aggregation steps recorded. The simulator shall retain the underlying quantities and rates so that a reported total can be audited and recomputed.

The activity model shall consider data gathering, data interaction, data storage, data processing, data servitization, data maintenance/repair, and backup deployment. Resource inputs shall be recorded where applicable, including personnel, machines, equipment, materials, facilities, and services. Results shall be classifiable across IT infrastructure, resources, data activities, and investment; investment shall distinguish capital expenditure (CapEx) from operational expenditure (OpEx). The cited paper focuses on manufacturing DT development, so its activity-based method shall be adapted to this flood-response SoS and its cost drivers; its manufacturing-specific prices shall not be copied as rescue-system prices.

**REQ-22 — Define cost drivers and rates.** Each cost element shall specify its category, resource/activity, cost driver and unit, rate or range, time horizon, and source/assumption. Examples of scenario-sensitive drivers include sensor/data volume and sampling frequency, messages exchanged, storage duration, processing workload, service availability, maintenance/repair effort, reserve standby/availability time, backup boat deployment time, and DT mission duration. Repair costs shall scale with repair effort; backup-use costs shall scale with the time a reserve boat is deployed for rescue; and standby costs shall be included when keeping a repaired DT or unused backup Disengaged and available consumes modeled resources. Profile-related cost differences shall result from modeled resource or activity consumption, not an unsupported flat surcharge. Uncertain rates shall support low/base/high estimates or sensitivity analysis.

**REQ-23 — Separate recurring and shared costs.** Costs that accrue with service duration or repeated data activities shall increase with the corresponding time, data volume, or activity count. One-time development, equipment, and other shared costs shall be reported separately or amortized across an explicit service life/mission count so they are not charged in full to every run. DT-time shall remain a separate utilization metric, reported in DT-minutes/hours and broken down by asset or role where useful; it is not a monetary cost estimate.

**REQ-24 — Compare cost and mission performance.** Reports shall compare estimated cost with success rate, rescued-victim outcomes, and DT-time for each strategy/configuration. They shall support cost-versus-success trade-off and Pareto analysis. The Dynamic strategy may be identified as preferable only if the observed results support that conclusion under declared assumptions; the simulator shall not encode “mixed is best” as a rule.

## 9. Repeated runs and reporting

**REQ-25 — Repeat stochastic runs.** Each experimental condition shall be executed with multiple random seeds. The number of runs and seed policy shall be recorded. Policy strategies shall be reported as Static permissive, Static restrictive, and Dynamic; internal identifiers may be retained for backward compatibility.

**REQ-26 — Record run-level data.** Each run shall record its policy strategy, profile configuration and parameters, health-state task success probabilities, rescue-sensitivity condition and parameter values with units, random seed, simulation time unit, metric time unit, step, mission service horizon, victim arrival/deadline schedule, flood input/model parameters, mission outcome, victims rescued before deadline, peak DT count, attached and Active DT-time in DT-minutes, pumping-station Active/service time in minutes, pump output, water level (m), flooded area (ha), storage and water volumes (m³), and health-related communication-unavailable time. Task-attempt outcomes shall be recorded by task, assigned role, health state, and outcome (success, health-related failure, physical failure, or other failure), including rescue tasks. The run shall also record estimated cost and cost components, health transitions, Poisson request outcomes and rate units, capacity deferrals, repair requests/time/completions, health-departure replacement time, backup requests/activations/deployment time, reserve Disengaged/Ideal availability, activation through Prepared, reserve return transitions, recovery/reserve-return outcomes, boat availability, and relevant state transitions.

**REQ-27 — Report aggregate comparisons.** For each policy strategy, profile configuration, and rescue-sensitivity condition, reports shall include success rate, rescued-victim outcomes, peak DT count, DT-time, estimated cost by category, pump output, and run count. Run-level results shall be retained to support variability and sensitivity analysis.

**REQ-28 — Diagnose strategy effects by departure-request rate.** Analysis outputs shall compare results separately for each policy strategy and autonomous departure-request-rate condition, rather than pooling rate conditions when attributing an effect to a strategy. The comparison shall report health-related departures, health replacement requests and activations, backup activations, repair requests and completions, rescue-task attempts and outcomes by health state, victims rescued before their deadlines, and mission success rate. These are observational metrics: collecting or plotting them shall not alter profile assignment, transition handling, task dispatch, or the mission-success predicate.

## 10. Parameters to calibrate

The baseline synthetic inputs in REQ-19 are selected for reproducibility, but are not calibrated to a particular flood site. They must not be interpreted as real-world measurements until supported by suitable data. The following assumptions and model parameters still require calibration or sensitivity analysis:

- water-level thresholds for Dynamic profile changes;
- site-specific flood inflow/hydrograph, storage geometry, and pump discharge, with hydrological units/data sources established before physical interpretation;
- the shared flood-water threshold, exposure increment, and exposure interval that advance physical health states;
- Poisson request rates and profile/role-dependent acceptance, denial, or delay behavior;
- boat rescue throughput as a function of flooded area;
- victim arrival demand, survival deadline, and mission success target;
- reserve-boat availability and replacement policy;
- number of repetitions per experimental condition;
- activity/resource cost drivers, cost rates or ranges, the mission service horizon, and allocation/amortization assumptions.

These parameters shall be recorded with experimental results. The following must be calibrated or explicitly labelled as synthetic before making physical claims: incident horizon and step size; flood inflow/rainfall hydrograph; storage and flooded-area relation; pump discharge and operating conditions; victim arrival schedule and survival deadline; task duration/throughput; and health-exposure thresholds. Hydrological inputs should use an identified event or a documented synthetic hydrograph; appropriate official historical hydrometric observations may be used where available. Profile comparisons shall keep the exogenous incident and victim demand matched. Calibration must not involve manual changes to files in `generated/`.

The cost framework is adapted from Su, Nassehi, McClenaghan, Langridge, and Hicks (2025), “A methodology for estimating the cost of a digital twin,” *Journal of Manufacturing Systems*, 80, 841–858. The paper applies activity-based costing to DT data activities and allocates resource costs across activities and cost categories. Its manufacturing-specific cost values are not directly transferable to flood-response operations. [Publisher article and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmsy.2025.04.004).
